How do Security Vault account protections work?

Security Vault uses several controls to protect account access. Each control has a different job, so it helps to understand how they work together.

Sign-in and vault access

Your account password is used to sign in. Your master password unlocks protected vault areas. Security Vault stores these passwords as bcrypt hashes. Vault sessions expire, and repeated failed unlock attempts are limited.

Optional two-factor authentication

When enabled, authenticator-based two-factor authentication asks for a time-based code during sign-in. That adds a step beyond your account password. Keep your recovery options up to date before you rely on it.

Biometric access on supported devices

On a supported mobile device, you can enable biometric login and vault unlock. Your device performs the biometric check. Biometric access is an account convenience feature; it does not make server-stored vault content end-to-end encrypted.

Sessions, locking and activity

Vault auto-lock reduces the time an unlocked vault remains available. Active-session controls let you review and revoke sign-ins, while security activity and alerts help you notice account events. If a device is lost, review your sessions and update your account credentials promptly.

Recovery and encryption limits

Account recovery can help restore access according to the available verification options. The service still holds the key for server-side encryption of vault content; learn exactly what that means on our Security page.
